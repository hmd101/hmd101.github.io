I am a Master's student in Machine Learning at the [University of Tübingen](https://uni-tuebingen.de/en/), currently working with [Brenden Lake](https://cims.nyu.edu/~brenden/) on my thesis research. I previously earned a Master's in Psychology at [New York University (NYU)](https://www.nyu.edu/) as a [Fulbright scholar](https://www.fulbrightprogram.org/) (2025), where I first joined Brenden's lab and completed my NYU thesis together with Prof. [Bob Rehder](https://psych.nyu.edu/rehder/). I also hold a Bachelor's degree in Cognitive Science from the University of Tübingen (2022) where I spent semester abroad in Taiwan and Hong Kong.

I am broadly interested in the computational ingredients of intelligence—both human and artificial. I am particularly interested in questions such as: What enables humans to learn from just a few examples and generalize efficiently and flexibly to new situations—something today's state-of-the-art AI systems still struggle with? My current work focuses on causal reasoning in humans and large language models (LLMs). I am especially excited about using machine learning methods to deepen our understanding of human cognition, while drawing on insights from psychology to design more intelligent and reliable agentic AI systems—capable of goal-directed behavior, flexible adaptation, and decision-making that better aligns with human expectations.

A key part of this effort involves quantifying uncertainty in reasoning and decision-making, enabling AI systems to know what they don't know. By integrating principled uncertainty into their reasoning, these systems can learn to defer, abstain, or seek more information when appropriate—making them not only more capable, but also safer, more trustworthy, and more aligned with human expectations.

In my free time, I enjoy travelling by bicycle and playing any sports that involves a ball.
